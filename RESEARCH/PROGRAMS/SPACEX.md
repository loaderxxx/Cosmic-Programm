# SpaceX — Falcon / Dragon / Starship / Starlink Technical Dossier

## Transportation architecture
SpaceX combines reusable launch vehicles, spacecraft, high-flight-rate operations and large communications infrastructure.

## Falcon 9
Falcon 9 is a two-stage LOX/RP-1 launcher. Its first stage uses nine Merlin engines and is designed for recovery and reuse; the second stage uses a Merlin Vacuum engine. Recovery hardware includes grid fins and landing systems.

## Dragon
Dragon provides cargo and crew transportation to low Earth orbit and supports ISS operations. Its role demonstrates a reusable spacecraft integrated with commercial launch operations.

## Starship / Super Heavy
SpaceX describes Starship/Super Heavy as a fully reusable transportation system for Earth orbit, Moon, Mars and beyond. Current published specifications describe a 124 m integrated vehicle, 9 m diameter, and 100+ t payload target in fully reusable configuration. Super Heavy uses 33 Raptor methane/oxygen engines; Starship uses three Raptor and three Raptor Vacuum engines.

## Reusability and propellant transfer
The architecture depends on rapid reuse, launch-site recovery/catch, tanker flights and orbital propellant transfer. SpaceX states that on-orbit refilling is intended to support high-energy missions to Mars.

## 2026 development direction
SpaceX's 2026 Starship V3 update describes changes to grid fins, hot staging, cryogenic systems, avionics, docking and ship-to-ship propellant transfer. The company reports extensive integrated flight testing and cryogenic propellant transfer tests.

## Starlink
Starlink provides a large-scale communications constellation and is also relevant to deep-space communications experiments and future lunar communications concepts.

## Engineering observations
The key system-level idea is not one vehicle but a reusable transportation stack: mass production + high test cadence + recovery + orbital refilling + standardized interfaces. This makes launch frequency and logistics part of the architecture rather than merely operations.
