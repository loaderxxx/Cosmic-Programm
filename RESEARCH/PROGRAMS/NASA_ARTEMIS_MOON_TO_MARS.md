# NASA Artemis / Moon to Mars — Technical Dossier

## Scope
NASA's current Moon to Mars architecture is organized into Human Lunar Return, Foundational Exploration, Sustained Lunar Evolution, and Humans to Mars. It decomposes the architecture into 12 technical sub-architectures: autonomous systems/robotics; communications/PNT; data systems; habitation; human systems; ISRU; infrastructure; logistics; mobility; power; transportation; utilization.

## Core elements
- SLS + Orion: crew transportation from Earth toward the Moon.
- Gateway: cislunar multi-purpose infrastructure for lunar missions and future deep-space operations.
- Human Landing Systems: commercial landers transfer crew between lunar orbit and surface.
- CLPS: commercial delivery model for science and technology payloads.
- Surface systems: EVA, suits, rovers, power, communications, logistics and utilization.

## 2026 context
NASA's current public Artemis plan identifies Artemis III as a planned 2027 low-Earth-orbit demonstration of commercial human landing systems, followed by a targeted first lunar landing in Artemis IV in 2028. Artemis V is described as a 2028 surface mission with an anticipated annual cadence thereafter.

## Technical architecture pattern
Earth launch -> crew spacecraft -> cislunar infrastructure -> commercial lunar lander -> surface systems -> logistics/utilization -> progressively more sustained lunar operations -> Mars precursor capabilities.

## Engineering observations
The architecture treats transportation, communications, power, logistics, autonomy, habitation and ISRU as coupled capability layers rather than isolated spacecraft. NASA's architecture documents explicitly map objectives to elements and technology gaps.
