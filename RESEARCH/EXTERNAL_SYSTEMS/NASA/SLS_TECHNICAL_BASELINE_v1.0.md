# NASA SLS — Technical Baseline v1.0

## System role
Super-heavy launch vehicle for crew and cargo missions beyond low Earth orbit.

## Architecture
- Core stage: cryogenic liquid hydrogen/liquid oxygen.
- Four RS-25 engines.
- Two five-segment solid rocket boosters.
- Upper stage and Orion integrated for Artemis missions.
- Block 1 is the initial crew configuration; later configurations add higher-energy capability.

## Core-stage engineering data
- length: approximately 64.6 m
- diameter: 8.4 m
- empty mass: approximately 85,275 kg
- liquid hydrogen capacity: approximately 317,000 lb
- liquid oxygen capacity: approximately 1.86 million lb
- nominal core-stage operation: approximately 500 s before separation
- Block 1 payload beyond lunar orbit: NASA states >27 metric tonnes.

## Avionics
The core stage houses flight computers and avionics. NASA describes three flight computers with two-of-three redundancy and extensive systems-integration testing.

## Manufacturing
Major structures are produced at Michoud. Final outfitting/integration uses Kennedy facilities. Structural and full-stage testing is part of the qualification approach.

## Engineering lessons
1. Heavy lift is inseparable from ground infrastructure.
2. Structural qualification is performed at representative scale.
3. Redundancy is designed into flight computing.
4. Vehicle architecture must be integrated with crew spacecraft and ground systems.
