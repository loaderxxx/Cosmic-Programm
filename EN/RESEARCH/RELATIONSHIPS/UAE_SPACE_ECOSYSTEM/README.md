## Decision supported

Identify who can supply a missing capability to whom, what evidence supports that relationship, and what the smallest responsible validation would be. This is not a list of promised partners, an official procurement directory or a claim of institutional endorsement.

The [typed graph](../../../../RESEARCH/RELATIONSHIPS/UAE_SPACE_ECOSYSTEM/GRAPH.json) separates published relationships from ten cooperation hypotheses. The [source register](../../../../RESEARCH/RELATIONSHIPS/UAE_SPACE_ECOSYSTEM/SOURCES.json) records publisher, publication/event date and access status. An official announcement is evidence of what the institution reports; it is not an independent audit of performance.

## 1. How the system fits together

**INTERPRETATION:** the relevant unit is a chain of capabilities, not a company name: public objectives → scientific or service requirements → spacecraft/payload → manufacturing and qualification → launch → ground operations → data product → user validation → recurring demand. A country-level ecosystem can coordinate this chain through several institutions and suppliers rather than one vertically integrated company.

Keep the programme authority, mission implementer, supplier, operator, data distributor, research user and funding mechanism as separate roles. Never infer that an agency controls every commercial actor. One organization may occupy several roles, but evidence is required for each role.

## 2. Who could help whom — hypotheses, not agreements

**H01 — Optical/SAR complementarity.** MBRSC-related optical data and Space42-related SAR data could support a comparative civil Earth-observation study. Potential benefit: assess whether combining different measurements improves a defined user decision. Failure modes: mismatched acquisition times, resolution, calibration, geographic coverage and licensing. First test: one documented use case and a held-out reference set; report the separate and combined results, including failure cases. No MBRSC–Space42 integration agreement is inferred.

**H02 — Academic validation of downstream analytics.** The University of Dubai laboratory could be a relevant evaluator of an EO analytics workflow; GIQ is a candidate distribution environment, not a required dependency. Value to a platform or startup would be a reproducible, independent benchmark, not another unsourced feature description. First test: publish a lawful dataset specification, evaluation code and an error report without claiming the institution's participation.

**H03 — Manufacturing research versus flight qualification.** Khalifa University's ADAM capabilities and FADA's advertised satellite engineering scope suggest a possible materials/process qualification research interface. [X04][X06] This does not prove joint work. First test: a public requirements-to-test matrix for one non-sensitive component class. A laboratory result becomes flight-qualification evidence only through the customer's accepted configuration, procedures and acceptance criteria.

**H04/H05 — COSMOSYNTH as a research contributor.** A useful contribution to an academic counterpart would be a reproducible Mars or civil EO analysis, a provenance-complete bibliography and an explicit account of limitations. Preparing such an artifact is different from having a research partnership. Do not describe research speed, accuracy or deployed AI as validated before measurement.

**H06/H07 — Existing services before a custom platform.** Copernicus is a candidate baseline-data route with dataset-specific terms and service quotas. AWS Ground Station's digital twin can validate contact workflows after onboarding, but does not deliver telemetry or payload data. [X23][X24] An offline, synthetic workflow is the initial test; paid accounts, data purchases and actual spacecraft commanding are outside this publication.

**H08/H09/H10 — Interface and evidence support.** Ground vendors and mission developers could benefit from a clear requirements/interface/evidence ledger. COSMOSYNTH can test this approach on public documents before proposing assistance. The output must expose gaps rather than invent supplier allocations, compatibility or qualification results.

## 3. Complementarity, alternatives and ownership

A data marketplace is not a spacecraft bus; a launch service is not lunar surface delivery; a ground scheduler is not a modem; a laboratory is not an accredited flight-acceptance authority. Select an architecture first, then compare suppliers within the same role.

**INTERPRETATION:** MBRSC, Space42, Orbitworks and FADA may overlap in parts of the national industrial landscape while serving different customer, mission and production roles. Treat them neither as interchangeable suppliers nor as a pre-existing integrated consortium. Compare contract scope, demonstrated configuration, acceptance evidence, data rights, lifecycle cost, support and dependencies.

An optical product and SAR product may be complementary for one question but redundant or unsuitable for another. A government-backed capability does not imply free access. A group-owned subsidiary does not add independent supply-chain resilience merely by having a different brand.

## 5. Three validation projects

These are **proposed tests**, not funded pilots, accepted partnerships or completed results. They require no external outreach to prepare.

### P1 — Reproducible civil EO benchmark

**Who / why:** COSMOSYNTH as an independent research contributor; an academic or downstream-data counterpart may value reproducibility. **Problem:** a feature claim does not demonstrate better decisions. **Evidence basis:** X03, X09–X11, X24. **Pilot:** choose one civilian environmental question; fix a held-out geographic/time split; compare a transparent baseline with a candidate method. **Required inputs:** lawful data, reference labels, an agreed metric and documented compute limits. **Acceptance:** report data exclusions, uncertainty, reproducibility instructions and per-case errors; success thresholds must be agreed before running. **Next step:** verify the dataset and publish the test specification. No achieved accuracy or institution acceptance is claimed.

### P2 — Mission requirements and supplier-evidence ledger

**Who / why:** COSMOSYNTH; an engineering or academic counterpart could use a transparent gap map. **Problem:** sources conflate capabilities, agreements and operational maturity. **Evidence basis:** X01–X02, X18–X21, X25–X26. **Pilot:** select one public subsystem and map each requirement to an interface, evidence item, owner role and test. **Required inputs:** public documents and unambiguous configuration/version identifiers. **Acceptance:** no unsupported “verified” row, every numerical claim traceable, every unresolved item actionable. **Next step:** produce a specimen ledger; do not present it as supplier certification.

### P3 — Software-only ground/contact workflow

**Who / why:** COSMOSYNTH; future mission-software collaborators may value repeatable integration tests. **Problem:** scheduling, telemetry transport and command authority are easily conflated. **Evidence basis:** X23, X25. **Pilot:** offline synthetic contacts, conflicts, timeouts and event logging; simulate telemetry separately. **Required inputs:** synthetic data, a state model and explicit non-flight boundaries. **Acceptance:** deterministic replay, rejected conflicting allocations, complete event trace and no external commands. **Next step:** write the executable test specification; AWS onboarding or paid services require a separate decision.

This release adds source-bounded relationships, complete EN/RU/AR analysis and concrete validation questions. It does **not** establish full national ecosystem coverage, private contacts, current contract prices, all corporate ownership chains, independent performance audits or complete translation of the entire historical repository. Publication update: the separate 55-company CABSAT/SATExpo English catalogue is now integrated into `main`; its detailed RU/AR translations are not included in this completion claim.
