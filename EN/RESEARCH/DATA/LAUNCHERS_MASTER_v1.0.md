# Launch Vehicles Master Dataset v1.0

## Purpose

Normalize public launch-vehicle data for comparison with Cosmic Programm requirements.

## NASA SLS
- Purpose: heavy lift for lunar and deep-space missions.
- Block 1: approximately 98.1 m; stated capability to deliver more than 27 t to deep space.
- Architecture: core stage, 4 RS-25 engines, 2 five-segment solid rocket boosters and the ICPS upper stage.
- Reusability: not provided for the launch vehicle's main elements.
- Engineering interest: integration of a super-heavy launcher with a crewed spacecraft and comprehensive ground preparation.

## SpaceX Falcon 9
- 70 m;
- diameter 3.7 m;
- launch mass 549,054 kg;
- published LEO payload 22,800 kg;
- 9 Merlin engines on the first stage;
- LOX/RP-1;
- recoverable first stage.
Source: official SpaceX materials.

## SpaceX Starship
- approximately 124 m;
- diameter 9 m;
- published payload target 100+ t;
- methane/oxygen;
- two-stage architecture;
- Super Heavy + Starship;
- fully reusable concept.
Source: official SpaceX materials and User's Guide.

## Blue Origin New Glenn
- more than 98 m;
- 7 m fairing;
- published 45 t to LEO and more than 13 t to GTO;
- 7 BE-4 engines on the first stage;
- 2 BE-3U engines on the upper stage;
- first stage designed for multiple flights.
Source: Blue Origin.

## ESA Ariane 6
- Ariane 62 and Ariane 64;
- published approximately 10.3 / 21.6 t to LEO;
- approximately 4.5 / 11.5 t to GTO;
- common diameter approximately 5.4 m;
- Vulcain 2.1 core engine;
- configurations differ in the number of P120C boosters.
Source: ESA.

## Parameters for further normalization

For every launch vehicle:
- mass;
- height;
- diameter;
- payload by orbit;
- engine;
- propellant;
- thrust;
- specific impulse;
- burn duration;
- number of stages;
- reuse;
- allowable centre-of-gravity range;
- vibration;
- acoustics;
- accelerations;
- payload interface;
- launch cost/cadence, only where reliable public data exists.
