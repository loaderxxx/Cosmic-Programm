# Lunar Systems Master Dataset v1.0

## Architectural functions

A lunar system must be viewed as a combination of:

1. delivery;
2. landing;
3. power;
4. communications;
5. navigation;
6. computing;
7. payloads;
8. mobility;
9. resource prospecting;
10. extraction and processing;
11. storage;
12. servicing;
13. logistics;
14. ground control.

## NASA

The Moon-to-Mars Architecture uses functional decomposition encompassing transportation, mobility, power, communications/navigation, resource utilization, infrastructure and other subsystems.

## China

The Chang'e / ILRS architecture illustrates a sequence of robotic lunar missions, resource prospecting and infrastructure development.

## ESA

ESA's lunar architecture includes robotic logistics and concepts for delivering cargo to the surface.

## Cosmic

The first lunar missions must not assume the existence of a fully developed base. The sequence should be:

prospecting → environmental confirmation → delivery of small infrastructure → long-duration operations → repeated logistics → infrastructure expansion → resource experiments.

## Principal engineering uncertainties

- site selection;
- illumination;
- temperature cycle;
- radiation;
- dust;
- communications with Earth;
- navigation;
- power;
- landing accuracy;
- long-duration autonomy.
