# From capabilities to system options

**Eight illustrations, not eight working products.** This reader summarizes the existing [public configuration study](https://github.com/loaderxxx/Cosmic-Programm/blob/75066d88bbcc96eb63f6242df48cbcf5eac9fc67/EN/RESEARCH/INDUSTRY/CABSAT_SATEXPO_2026/SYSTEM_LAYER_COMBINATIONS.md). Named organizations are research candidates from that snapshot; alternatives are choices, not additional mandatory suppliers. [Open their dossiers](../ORGANIZATIONS/README.md).

## OPT-01 — Virtual spacecraft and ground-contact lifecycle

The source study places Antaris, Calian and AWS at different mission-model, resource-scheduling and managed-contact layers. The research question is how spacecraft state, contact windows and recovery events should interact. **Boundary:** mission command authority and ground-resource ownership must be explicit; a scheduling simulation is not a telemetry-delivery test. **Smallest test:** an offline orbit/contact scenario with two synthetic stations and one resource outage, producing a reproducible event log.

## OPT-02 — Owned ground station versus managed service

The study compares ground engineering, monitoring, antennas and RF distribution, with Calian, SatService, AvL and ETL examples; managed services and other integrators are alternatives. **Boundary:** compare complete operating responsibilities, corporate relationships and interfaces, not isolated product names. **Smallest test:** an interface checklist and an ownership/support comparison without hardware procurement or RF transmission.

## OPT-03 — Digital ground-signal processing

ETL and INTEGRASYS appear in the source discussion of digitization and signal analysis; other providers are alternatives. **Boundary:** a shared DIFI/VITA family does not establish compatibility of packet profiles, timestamps, context and sample formats. **Smallest test:** public or synthetic conformance vectors and malformed-packet checks, explicitly not a validation of vendor hardware.

## OPT-04 — Commercial VSAT network

The study separates satellite service, one modem/hub family, terminals and installation/monitoring tools. SES, iDirect, AvL and INTEGRASYS are examples, with other operators and modem families as alternatives. **Boundary:** select a coherent configuration; five modem families are not five necessary components. **Smallest test:** compare one scenario's interfaces, coverage assumptions, waveform and service prerequisites before a technical or commercial commitment.

## OPT-05 — Small spacecraft and Earth-observation data

The study links possible roles for Geoscan, Antaris, Calian and Neo Space Group in platform, mission modelling, ground operations and downstream data. **Boundary:** an imagery marketplace is not automatically connected to a chosen spacecraft, and access or data rights cannot be assumed. **Smallest test:** define one user question, its data requirements and one permitted dataset before specifying integration.

## OPT-06 — High-latitude and mobile connectivity

Space Norway, AvL and IEC Telecom are examples of infrastructure, terminal and integration roles in the source study; other service options are conditional alternatives. **Boundary:** distinguish geography, orbit, service availability and commercial versus restricted payload access. **Smallest test:** a coverage-and-interface comparison for one openly defined use case, not a claim that service is available to us.

## OPT-07 — Gulf regional satellite, IoT and media services

The source study uses Es'hailSat and GulfSat-related reports to examine capacity, service provision and regional alternatives. **Boundary:** distinguish a dated service report or memorandum from a new spacecraft integration or COSMOSYNTH relationship. **Smallest test:** document the service chain, customer need, source dates and unresolved access conditions.

## OPT-08 — Capability development and industrial interfaces

Ground engineering, virtual mission testing and industry guidance form an illustrative learning-and-validation route. The source study uses Calian, Antaris and the World Teleport Association as examples. **Boundary:** industry organizations, regulators, mission centres and vendors have different roles; no affiliation or accreditation is implied. **Smallest test:** a jointly scoped, non-confidential exercise with a designated technical reviewer.

## How an option becomes a pilot

**Question → function → candidate role → alternative → interface → evidence gap → test → review.** Until the responsible counterpart, usable data, acceptance criterion and resources are established, an option remains a hypothesis.
